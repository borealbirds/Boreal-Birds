Each fitted bootstrap model was applied to the environmental raster stack to predict male density at a 1-km resolution. This produced 32 prediction surfaces for each eligible species, modelling region, and prediction year.

Regional predictions overlapped because models were trained and predicted within buffered regions. Within each country, overlapping predictions were combined using distance-weighted blending: predictions received greater weight toward the interior of their modelling region and less weight near its buffered edge. This reduced abrupt transitions at regional boundaries.

Distance weighting was used to create mosaics for Canada, Alaska, and the hemiboreal Lower 48. Environmental extrapolation was not used to select, remove, or weight predictions during mosaicking; it is distributed separately as an interpretive diagnostic.

Version 5.0 predictions should be interpreted together with their uncertainty and model-support information.

- Sampling remains uneven across the study area. Use the detection-distance and training- and testing-sample density layers to identify areas with limited observational support.
- Environmental extrapolation identifies places where prediction conditions were not well represented in model training. Extrapolated predictions may be less reliable even when bootstrap standard deviation is low.
- Bootstrap standard deviation measures variation generated by the resampling workflow; it does not capture every source of uncertainty.
- Species-range and data-extent limits reduce unsupported predictions, but boundaries remain uncertain where detections or surveys are sparse.
- Predictor availability and retained predictor sets differ among modelling regions, and habitat relationships may vary regionally.
- Predictor importance and partial dependence describe fitted associations, not causal ecological effects.

Predictions represent expected male-bird density, expressed as male birds per hectare at a 1-km resolution, and should not be interpreted as direct counts at individual locations.
